Race inputs, sources & how it works
Forecasts checked November 2, 2026

DDHQ: 62% Democratic control · Midterm Forecast: 76%. Different inputs, uncertainty and independent-caucus assumptions produce different odds. These are forecasts, not poll vote shares.

Our simulation has no forced chamber winner or target probability. Most race centers use Midterm Forecast’s November 2 model margins; Nebraska, South Carolina and South Dakota instead use 270toWin polling averages, normalized to the two modeled candidates. Montana uses its three-candidate polling average, normalized to 100%. Polls disagree and sparse polling adds uncertainty. Minor candidates and undecideds are omitted; normalization redistributes them proportionally. This is a simplified simulation, not a reproduction of any forecaster’s model or the full ballot.

The live needle reruns 2,000 possible outcomes using counted votes, baseline shares and confirmed calls, never the hidden sampled final totals. Its estimates blend early returns with the prior and become more certain as ballots are counted. Shared shifts are estimated from reporting states. Leans begin at 65% model confidence, likely at 85%; below 10% counted is too early to call. Estimates are approximate and may move sharply as batches arrive. Each run adds shared national error (3-point standard deviation) and state error (4 points, or 6 for sparse-poll independent races). Montana separately varies each of its three shares (6-point standard deviation), then normalizes; its sparse, conflicting polls warrant wider uncertainty. All errors are centered at zero. Candidate vote totals are fixed at the start and reported in batches after polls close. Calls occur when the leader cannot be overtaken by any remaining ballots. Turnout and counting speed are illustrative; ranked-choice voting, runoff elections, recounts and multi-day counting are omitted.

The 34 Democratic-caucus held seats already include King and Sanders. New independent winners remain gold and are listed separately; this optional assumption changes control calculations only. Democrats need 51; Republicans need 50 with the vice president. Otherwise, independents hold the balance. Editable percentages below are modeled vote-share centers, not raw polling percentages. Editing them updates our estimate without changing other states.
