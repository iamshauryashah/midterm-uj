03 — THE FINE PRINT

A snapshot, not a crystal ball.

The fourth map uses the latest verified release from each of Siena and CNN/SSRS in this snapshot. Where both pollsters cover a state, candidate support is averaged with equal weight (one latest poll per pollster), without recency or sample-size weighting. The leader and margin come from those averaged shares. Where only one pollster covers a state, that poll is used. A candidate missing from one poll is averaged only across polls reporting that candidate and marked “1 poll”; missing support is never treated as zero. Montana instead uses a user-provided screenshot with no pollster or dates supplied. Individual source results and release dates appear in the table and state details. Where neither pollster is available, it assumes the nominee of the party favored by Cook Political Report’s October 30 ratings wins. These states have striped fills and an “expected” label in the table. This is our scenario using those ratings, not a Cook prediction for each candidate. Tied polls and unrated or toss-up races without polls stay uncalled.

How to read this dashboard

Map 1 colors the party holding the specific seat on the ballot, not the state’s entire delegation. Florida and Ohio are special elections. Personal picks start blank and save in this browser only.

Simulator: Each button press draws a new election from the same October 31 snapshot. Georgia starts at Ossoff +8 and North Carolina at Cooper +12, as user-provided simulator settings. Other two-contender polled races start from the margin between the two highest-polling candidates. Without a poll, it assigns an illustrative margin of 20 points for Solid ratings, 10 for Likely, 5 for Lean, or 0 for Toss-up, toward the favored party. In Random swing mode, it draws a shared national shift (normal distribution, standard deviation 3 points). Set party swing mode replaces that draw with your chosen value from −20 to +20 margin points. Both modes add a separate random state shift (standard deviation 4 points). The candidate with the resulting positive margin wins. These are our modeling assumptions, not Siena, CNN/SSRS, or Cook win probabilities, and are not calibrated forecasts.

The simulator models three contenders in Montana and Alaska, and two elsewhere. Montana uses the user-provided screenshot: Alme 30%, Bodnar 26%, Bankhead 21%. The polling map and table retain those shares and show Alme +4. Pollster and fieldwork dates were not supplied. The simulator normalizes the 77% reported for those three candidates to 100%, excluding the remaining 23%. Alaska uses an explicitly fictional three-way baseline: Peltola 53%, incumbent Dan S. Sullivan 46%, and Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. 1%. The last candidate is distinct from the Republican incumbent. For three-way races, half the national-plus-state margin shift is transferred from the Republican share to the non-Republican candidates, divided in proportion to their starting shares. Independent-vs-Democrat variation has standard deviation 2 share points in Montana and 0.75 in Alaska. Negative shares are bounded and normalized; rounded shares sum to 100%. The plurality leader wins these simulated scenarios. Alaska ranked-choice transfers are not modeled. In unpolled races these are the Democratic and Republican nominees; in Idaho and South Dakota the Republican faces Todd Achilles and Brian Bengs respectively. Polled Nebraska includes independent Dan Osborn. The common shift moves the main non-Republican contenders together. Ranked-choice transfers, runoffs, turnout, and stale-poll uncertainty are not modeled. Only the modeled candidates can win. A simulated winner represents the eventual seat outcome. Click any state on map 5 to inspect that run’s winner, vote shares, margin, and total party swing with its national and state-only components. Positive swing values favor Democrats or the main non-Republican challenger; negative values favor Republicans. Swing is the change from the starting poll/rating margin, not a change from the previous election. A 2-point margin swing moves each contender’s share by 1 point. The simulator also displays net Senate seat changes against the current 47 Democratic-aligned and 53 Republican seats. Swing settings affect the next run; an existing run keeps the settings it used. For two-contender races, vote shares are derived as (100 + simulated margin) / 2 for one contender, with the remainder assigned to the other. They are bounded to 0–100% and rounded to 0.1%; a rounded 50–50 result is displayed as 50.1–49.9 in favor of the simulated winner. These are illustrative shares among the modeled contenders, not total-ballot shares or turnout. Independent winners remain in Other; no caucus is assumed.

Full-chamber totals include 65 seats not up for election: 31 Republican and 34 Democratic-aligned, including 2 independents. Other predicted winners are counted separately; no new independent’s caucus is assumed. A 50–50 Senate is subject to the vice president’s tie-breaking vote.

Poll shares are rounded, are not win probabilities, and may not total 100%. Close leads can fall within polling error. Dates shown are release dates; source links provide fieldwork and methodology. Alaska’s ranked-choice result cannot be inferred with certainty from first-choice polling.
